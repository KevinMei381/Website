Agria Potato

The Agria potato is a floury potato. It has lots of starch but little moisture. That means it can fall apart being boiled. Their best use is roasted potatoes, chips, fries and mashed potatoes. It is also an example of a late variety of potato which mean its take a long time to grow.

Jersey Benne Potato

The Jersey Benne potato is a waxy potato and doesn't fall apart when boiled because of their dense structure. These potatoes have high moisture and little starch. They can be boiled whole and good ingredient into making stew, soup or even a salad. Jersey Benne is a early variety of potato because it only takes less time to harvest.

Rua Potato

The Rua potato is an all-round potato and is a maincrop and takes medium time to grow. They have medium starch and moisture. Rua potatoes are half waxy and half floury. Because it's versatile and a all-rounder, it can be used for boiling, mashing and baking.

How to finally grow potatoes?

Step 1: Chit your potatoes

Buy seed potatoes as you check their condition and buy them about 1 month before planting.For early varieties like jersey benne, you want to start planing in late August to September. For maincrop like Rua andAgria, you want to start planting in October or Novemeber. Don't buy any that are too squishy, wrinkled, have black rot, mold or smells bad.

About 3 week before planting, you should leave your seed potatoes in an egg carton or similar. The carton would keep away grounded pests. Keep them in a cool(10°C to 15°C) bright spots thats out of direct sunlight until green sprouts grow to about 2cm long. Too hot and the potato seed would dry up. Too dark cause weak sprout to snap off and fall, inviting pests.

Place them with sprouts primarily facing up. Keep space between the potatoes to keep good airflow. Potatoes release a bit of moisture through the pores on their skin or else there will be no airflow bewtween them to get rid of the moisture. High humidity and moisture makes mold or fungal spores easier to grow and make the seed potatoes go bad

Step 2: Prepare the soil to plant

Start by choosing the day for planting. Jersey benne should be planted in late Auguts and September while Rua and Agria s recommended to be planted in October or November. Then choose a sunny spot that gets 6 to 8 hours if sunlight. Dig holes about 10-15cm deep make sure each of the seed potato sprouts face primarily up. You don't need to space pits if you just dig a long trnech.

For the early season potato, Jersey Benne, you want the pits about 30cm away form each other. For the maincrops like Rua and Agria you want about 40cm between pits. Potatoes love lots of organic matter because it helps them grow so you can add in some potato fertilizer. Then place a seed potato with the sprout facing up into each pit. Then bury them halfway with about 10cm of dirt and not buryinng them enitrily with 15cm of dirt.

Step 3: Start growing potatoes

Water them lightly when the top layer of soil is dry to touch. After watering the soil, it should be as wet as a wrung out sponge. Is the soil is woaking wet, then the seed potato might rot away. It usually takes about 10-14 days for early varieties(Jersey Benne) for shoots to grow from the ground. It takes about 2-3 weeks for maincrop and late varieties(Agria & Rua) for the shoots to grow out of the ground.

For early growth, you can start watering them with about 1.25 Litres of water per potato at the bottom of the stem to keep leaves dry. Water 1-2 times per week. The top soil layer should dry out between waterings but the soil 5cm down should remain damp. Wehn the stem grows to abotu 15-20cm you should mound up so sorrounding dirt 5-10cm tall onto the stem leaving the top stick out.

Step 4:Finish growing poatoes

Water each plant 2 to 3 times a week(weather dependent).Use a slow stream of 3-4 litres of water onto the base of the hilling or the small dirt hill for each plant. When the plant has grown another 15-20cm, then mound up more surrounding dirt to make the hill another 5-10cm. Only stop when the plant starts flowering.

Early varieties of potatoes finsh growing faster than the other types. The leaves and stem should stay green right till harvest. Just keep on watering the Jersey Benne until 1 day before harvest. The cue to dig them up is if the flowers start opening or fallign off which shoudl be 70-90 days after planting.

Maincrop like Rua take longer to harvest and the plant and leaves should turn yellow. Eventually the stem should fall down. When the leaves start yellowing cut the watering by half and water it only 1 time a week. When the stem flops, stop watering. Then wait 1-2 weeks before digging them up. The wait should harden their skin.

Late varieties like Agria grow until later summer or early autumn. In the late summer or early autumn, the leaves start turning yellow,then brown and then completly die off. As soon as the leaves start yellowing cut the watering entirly. Drepriving Agria will give it that fluffy dry texture. Leave it for 2-3 weeks and then harvest.
