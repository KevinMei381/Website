Growing potatoes?

Why should you start growing potatoes?

1. They easy to grow and don't require much maintenance or space.

2. A seed potato can turn into lots more potatoes.

3. Homegrown potatoes are fresher or can be stored for months.

4. Potatoes provide lots of carbohydrates

5. They can be prepared and seasoned in lots of ways.

6. You like eating potatoes.

Agria Potato

The Agria potato is a floury potato. It has lots of starch but little moisture. That means it can fall apart being boiled. Their best use is roasted potatoes, chips, fries and mashed potatoes. It is also an example of a late variety of potato which mean its take a long time to grow.

Jersey Benne Potato

The Jersey Benne potato is a waxy potato and doesn't fall apart when boiled because of their dense structure. These potatoes have high moisture and little starch. They can be boiled whole and good ingredient into making stew, soup or even a salad. Jersey Benne is a early variety of potato because it only takes less time to harvest.

Rua Potato

The Rua potato is an all-round potato and is a maincrop and takes medium time to grow. They have medium starch and moisture. Rua potatoes are half waxy and half floury. Because it's versatile and a all-rounder, it can be used for boiling, mashing and baking.

When you potatoes grow in the ground and when you harvest potatoes, the dense dirt around will crumble and become loose and airy. The airy earth will be already tilled and make planting the next crops very easy. I will also provide easy growth for later crop's roots. The potatoes plant's canopy will block sunlight which helps prevent weeds from growing.

After harvesting, the remaining plants/roots decomposes back into the dirt and the harvest is massive The overall positive is it helps the dirt temporarily and not long term.

Negatives

The negatives are that if you disturb the soil to much, you might destory the soil aggregates. Soil aggregates are clumps of sand, silt and clay stuck together by micorbial glues and plant roots so they resist erosion and resist being blown away. Potatoes use lots of nutrients to develop so the soil will need time to recover. Potatoes also attract pests.

There is also something called the Potato Blight which is a potato disease. Its a disease that make potatoes completely unedible and turn into a foul mush.

Because of the many types of potatoes (3 examples above), you can make a big variety of potatoes like baked Agria potatoes. They contain lots of carbohydrates so you can get lots of energy. Potatoes are also the 3rd place staple crop just under wheat and rice. A single medium sized potato contains high levels of Vitamin C (20-27mg), Vitamin B6 (0.5mg), and Potassium. It will help boost immune system, regulate mood, and keep the blood lines healthy.

uses: Home cooking

The negatives to boiling potatoes is it takes long and the water suck out nutrients from the potatoes. The solution is to steam, microwave, or roast potatoes. If potatoes are stored for too long, potatoes become green and toxic due to the Solanine. Solanine can cause nausea, headaches and/or stomach cramps.

Potatoes rely lots on seasoning to add flavour like butter and makes it unhealthy. The potato starch breaks down very quickly into sugar when you digest it. Plain boiled or mashed potatoes have a high Glycemic Index which causes fast surges in blood sugar levels if eaten without protein, healthy fats, or extra fiber.

Beginner guide to grow potatoes.

Step 1: Chit your potatoes

Buy seed potatoes as you check their condition and buy them about 1 month before planting.For early varieties like Jersey benne, you want to start planing in late August to September. For maincrop like Rua andAgria, you want to start planting in October or Novemeber. Don't buy any that are too squishy, wrinkled, have black rot, mold or smells bad.

About 3 week before planting, you should leave your seed potatoes in an egg carton or similar. The carton would keep away grounded pests. Keep them in a cool(10°C to 15°C) bright spots thats out of direct sunlight until green sprouts grow to about 2cm long. Too hot and the potato seed would dry up. Too dark cause weak sprout to snap off and fall, inviting pests.

Place them with sprouts primarily facing up. Keep space between the potatoes to keep good airflow. Potatoes release a bit of moisture through the pores on their skin or else there will be no airflow bewtween them to get rid of the moisture. High humidity and moisture makes mold or fungal spores easier to grow and make the seed potatoes go bad

Step 2: Prepare the soil to plant

Start by choosing the day for planting. Jersey benne should be planted in late August and September while Rua and Agria is recommended to be planted in October or November. Then choose a sunny spot that gets 6 to 8 hours if sunlight. Dig holes or a trench about 10-15cm deep make sure each of the seed potato sprouts face primarily up.

For the early season potato, Jersey Benne, you want the pits about 30cm away from each other. For the maincrops like Rua and Agria you want about 40cm between pits. Potatoes love lots of organic matter because it helps them grow so you can add in some potato fertilizer. Then place a seed potato with the sprout facing up into each pit. Then bury them halfway with about 10cm of dirt and not buryinng them enitrily with 15cm of dirt.

Step 3: Start growing potatoes

Water them lightly when the top layer of soil is dry to touch. After watering the soil, it should be as wet as a wrung out sponge. Is the soil is soaking wet, then the seed potato might rot away. It takes about 10-14 days for early varieties(Jersey Benne) and 2-3 weeks for maincrop and late varieties(Agria & Rua) for the shoots to grow out of the ground.

For early growth, you can start watering them with about 1.25 Litres of water per potato 1-2 times every week at the bottom of the stem to keep leaves dry.The top soil layer should dry out between waterings but the soil 5cm down should remain damp. When the stem grows to about 15-20cm you should mound up so surrounding dirt 5-10cm tall onto the stem leaving the top stick out.

Step 4:Finish growing potatoes

Water each plant 2 to 3 times a week and use a slow stream of 3-4 litres of water on the base of the hilling. When the plant has grown another 15-20cm, then mound up the mound another 5-10cm more. Only stop when the plant starts flowering. Early varieties of potatoes finish growing faster than the other types. The leaves and stem should stay green right till harvest. Keep watering Jersey Benne until 1 day before harvest. The cue to dig them up is if the flowers start opening or falling off which should be 70-90 days after planting.

Maincrop like Rua take longer to harvest and the plant and leaves should turn yellow. When the leaves start yellowing cut the watering by half and water it only 1 time a week until the stem falls. Then wait 1-2 weeks before digging them up. The wait should harden their skin. Late varieties like Agria grow until later summer or early autumn. In the late summer or early autumn, the leaves start turning yellow,then brown and then completly die off. As soon as the leaves start yellowing cut the watering entirely.
